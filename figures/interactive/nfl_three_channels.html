Probability the next drive scores, by field-position decile of the next drive's starting position, split by whether the prior own-defense-stop succeeded (1) or not (0). The vertical gap collapses inside each decile — most "momentum" is just short fields.

Reading guide. Panel A. Three channels enter every drive jointly. Two effects flip sign or direction once the dependent variable shifts from a binary score indicator (col 1–2) to drive-level EPA (col 3): the own-defensive-stop coefficient flips negative, and the own-offensive carryover stays negative throughout. Only opponent-suppression keeps a stable, large, negative effect across all three specs.
Panel B. Within each field-position decile, the score-rate gap between drives following a successful own-defensive stop (1) and drives following a failed stop (0) shrinks substantially compared with the unconditional gap. The remaining within-decile differences load mostly on the opponent-suppression channel, consistent with the headline result that cross-team momentum is the only channel that survives field-position conditioning.
Source: drive-level estimates from tables/tab_all_channels.tex (Panel A) and tables/fp_decile_rates.csv (Panel B). All standard errors clustered on game and possessing team. Hover any point for full numbers.
